# Core Brief

> **Template — replace this content with your own.** Every agent in every round reads this file, so it is the highest-leverage two pages in the repository. It is also the cheapest: two pages loaded nine times per round is negligible token cost, and it prevents nine agents from reasoning in a vacuum.
>
> Keep it to roughly two pages. When it grows past that, move detail into a topic file under the relevant `context/` folder and add a line to `INDEX.md`. This file holds only what *every* lens needs.

---

## Who we are

One paragraph. What the business does, at what scale, in which markets. Enough that an agent reading it cold can calibrate whether a "€50M opportunity" is transformative or a rounding error.

## Where we are now

Current position in plain terms — revenue scale, growth rate, margin structure, market position. Include the uncomfortable parts. An agent working from a flattering self-description produces flattering analysis, which is the opposite of useful.

## Current strategy

What we have committed to, and what we have explicitly decided *not* to do. The second half matters more than teams expect: it stops the panel from recommending a path already considered and rejected, and it tells an agent when a finding is genuinely new rather than a rediscovery.

## Standing constraints

The boundaries every recommendation must respect:

- **Financial** — hurdle rate, margin floor, leverage limit, maximum single-commitment size
- **Strategic** — segments, geographies, or business models that are off the table, and why
- **Operational** — capacity ceilings, supply commitments, contractual obligations
- **Organisational** — change capacity already committed, hiring constraints
- **Regulatory** — regimes we operate under, commitments made to regulators

Give the reason alongside each constraint. A constraint with a stated reason can be intelligently challenged when circumstances change; a bare rule just gets worked around.

## How we make decisions

Who decides what, at what threshold, and on what cycle. This determines whether a recommendation is actionable now or has to wait for a committee that meets quarterly — and shapes how the Partner sequences the recommended actions.

## What we are watching

The two or three external variables that would most change our strategy if they moved, with the current reading on each. This is what the daily delta review checks against, so keep it current and keep it observable — a variable nobody can measure is not a trigger, it is a worry. At daily frequency an unobservable trigger is worse than at weekly: the delta review runs against it every single day and has nothing to report each time.
